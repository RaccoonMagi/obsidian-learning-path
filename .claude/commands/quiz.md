Quiz the user on material they've recently studied. Active recall under mild pressure.

**Step 1 — Scope the quiz**
Ask: "What do you want to be quizzed on?
(a) Everything from the current resource
(b) A specific topic
(c) Weakest areas — I'll pull from Feynman notes with confidence below 3"

If (c): check Feynman notes in the vault for `confidence: 1` or `confidence: 2` and focus there.

---

**Step 2 — Set the format**
Ask: "Pick a quiz style:
(a) I ask, you answer — feedback after each
(b) Rapid fire — all questions first, then review
(c) Predict the output — I show examples, you say what happens"

---

**Step 3 — Run the quiz**

Generate 5–10 questions calibrated to the user's current roadmap position and chosen topic.

**Question types (use all of these, weighted toward behavior/why):**
- What does [example] produce/output/evaluate to?
- Why does [behavior] happen?
- What is the difference between [A] and [B]?
- What goes wrong if you [common mistake]?
- Explain [concept] in one sentence without jargon
- What is the rule that governs [situation]?

Do not ask: "Define [term]." Pure definitions are the weakest form of knowledge.

---

**Step 4 — Score and debrief**

After all questions:
- Count correct / total
- For every wrong or hesitant answer: brief gap-fill (see `/gap-fill` for full protocol)
- Ask: "Which of these should become flashcards?"

Score interpretation:
- 9–10 / 10: Strong. Move to harder material or deeper edge cases.
- 7–8 / 10: Solid with targeted gaps to address.
- 5–6 / 10: The concepts need Feynman notes rewritten and more drilling.
- Under 5 / 10: Go back to source material. Study has not produced understanding yet.

---

**Step 5 — Recommend next action**

- Strong: "You're ready to move forward."
- Gaps found: "Write a Feynman note on [weakest topic] before continuing."
- Struggling: "Reread [specific section] using the Cornell method and come back."
