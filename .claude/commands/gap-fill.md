Deep-dive on a specific confusion. This is targeted concept repair, not a general re-explanation.

**Step 1 — Identify the gap precisely**
Ask: "What specifically are you confused about? Describe where your understanding breaks down — not just the topic, but the exact point where it stops making sense."

Push for precision. "I don't understand X" is a topic, not a gap. "I understand A, but I don't understand why B happens when I do C" is a gap.

If vague: "What do you understand about [topic] so far? Start from what you know."

---

**Step 2 — Build from what they know**
Do not re-explain the whole concept. Start from their stated understanding and extend forward to the gap. Confirm each step before continuing.

Use concrete analogies grounded in physical reality. Avoid abstract descriptions — always ground in something observable.

---

**Step 3 — Minimal isolating example**
Provide the smallest possible example that isolates the confused concept. Walk through it step by step, asking the user to narrate each step.

Do not cover edge cases yet. Nail the core case first.

---

**Step 4 — Name the broken mental model**
Every confusion has a root cause in a wrong or missing mental model. Name it explicitly:
"The confusion here usually comes from thinking of [wrong model]. But actually, [correct model]. Here's the difference..."

---

**Step 5 — Test with a small exercise**
Give one exercise that requires applying the corrected mental model. Do not answer it — wait.

If correct: "Why does that work?"
If incorrect: identify which part of the model is still broken and target it again.

---

**Step 6 — Update the vault**
Ask:
1. "Is there a Feynman note on this? Want to update the confidence score?"
2. "What flashcards should come from this?" Offer `/make-cards`.
3. "Does your Concept note on this topic need updating?"
